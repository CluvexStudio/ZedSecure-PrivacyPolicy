1. Who we are and what this covers

ZedSecure VPN ("the app") is published by Cluvex Studio ("we", "us"). This document covers the ZedSecure VPN Android application distributed under the application ID com.zedsecure.vpn.

ZedSecure is a client, not a VPN service. It does not include, bundle or sell access to any VPN server. It connects to servers, subscriptions and providers that you add — a configuration link you paste, a subscription URL you enter, a corporate gateway you configure, or a public network such as Tor or Psiphon that you choose to use. We have no visibility into, and no control over, any of them.

Because there is no account and no server of ours, there is no database in which your use of the app could be recorded.

2. What the app stores on your device

All of the following is written to the app's private storage on your device. It is not transmitted to us, and it is removed when you uninstall the app.

  • Server configurations and credentials you add — including passwords, private keys, pre-shared keys and certificates. Vault (.zsx) configurations are stored encrypted with AES-256-GCM.
  • Subscription URLs you enter, and the configurations fetched from them.
  • App settings: routing rules, DNS choices, per-app selections, appearance and personalisation.
  • Connection statistics: bytes sent and received, connection duration and measured latency, kept per profile so the app can show them.
  • Diagnostic logs, shown in the app's log screen. They are kept in memory and in the app's own files, are never uploaded automatically, and are shared only if you choose to copy or share them yourself. Logs can contain server addresses and connection errors, so review them before sharing.

3. What we do not collect

The app contains no analytics framework, no crash-reporting service, no advertising library and no tracking SDK of any kind. Specifically, we do not collect:

  • your browsing history, DNS queries, or the contents of your traffic;
  • your VPN server addresses, credentials, or subscription URLs;
  • your name, email address, phone number or any account identifier — there is no account;
  • your device's advertising ID, contacts, location, camera, microphone or files;
  • usage analytics, feature telemetry or crash reports.

We do not sell, rent or share personal data, because we do not receive any.

5. Permissions and why each is needed

PermissionWhy the app requests it

FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USETo keep the tunnel running with a visible ongoing notification while you use other apps, as Android requires.
ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE, CHANGE_NETWORK_STATETo detect connectivity changes and reconnect when the network changes, and to configure the tunnel interface.

QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES Used solely by the per-app proxy feature, so the app can show you a list of your installed applications and let you choose which of them go through the tunnel. The list is read on your device, displayed to you, and never transmitted anywhere. Only the selection you make is saved locally. If you never open that screen, the list is never used.

The app does not request location, contacts, camera, microphone, SMS or storage-wide file access. The VPN connection itself is established through Android's standard system VPN consent dialog, which you must approve.

6. Device identifier

Settings shows a Device ID so you can copy it. This value is Android's ANDROID_ID — an identifier generated by the operating system that is specific to your device and this app's signing key, and that changes on a factory reset.

The app does not transmit this value anywhere. It is displayed for one purpose: if someone shares a locked Vault configuration with you and chooses to restrict it to specific devices, they need your Device ID in order to include yours. Sending it to them is an action you take deliberately, outside the app.

7. Security, and its honest limits

Vault configurations are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Ordinary configurations and settings are stored in the app's private storage, which Android isolates from other applications.

We would rather be accurate than reassuring, so:

  • A rooted or compromised device offers no guarantees. Anything the app can read, software with root access can also read. Vault's device binding is a deterrent against casual re-sharing — it is not DRM, and a technically capable person can work around it.
  • Your privacy depends on your provider. Whoever operates the server you connect through can see your traffic to the extent the protocol allows. Choose providers you trust.
  • No VPN makes you anonymous. It changes where your traffic appears to come from; it does not stop you being identified by accounts you sign into, or by your browser.
  • No transmission or storage method is perfectly secure. We aim for sound practice, not impossible promises.
